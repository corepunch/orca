# What children come to Encarta for

A design note for the home screen and the library. It looks at the
multimedia encyclopedias children used in the 1990s and 2000s, lists 100
things a child (and the adults beside them) want to do, and derives what the
main menu must make easy. Stories marked **now** work in the app today;
the rest are the backlog, roughly in order of value.

## What the classic children's encyclopedias got right

| Encyclopedia | What it did for finding things | What Encarta takes from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DK *Eyewitness* CD-ROMs (Nature, Science, History of the World) | The home screen is a place, not a list: a desk or cabinet of objects, each object a subject, drawers for activities, a quiz master. Every object is labelled. | The room with labelled exhibits and drawers (already here). |
| DK *Eyewitness History of the World* | History is browsed by **time** as well as by subject, so “before / after” is visible. | The **Time Machine**: everything that happened, in order, from dinosaurs to now. |
| *Кирилл и Мефодий* children's encyclopedias, *Аванта+* | Broad, school-shaped sections: Universe, Earth, Living Nature, Human, **History**, Culture & Art, Technology. History is a first-class wing, not a footnote. | History as its own wing of the home screen; the remaining wings planned below. |
| Encarta Kids / Britannica Kids / World Book Kids | Search box first, with forgiving matching and suggestions; topics grouped by school subject; read-aloud. | A search field that is always on the home screen, that ignores filler words and small spelling slips, and that suggests something when nothing matches. |
| *Was ist Was* books | Every page starts from a child's question. | Questions as the way into every article (already here). |

Two lessons decide the layout. First, children browse by **picture and
place** before they can spell what they want, so the room stays the heart of
the home screen. Second, a child who already knows what they want (“the US
civil war”, “Napoleon’s battle at Waterloo”) must reach it without learning
the room: search and the history wing sit on the home screen itself.

## The main menu, derived

1. **Ask box on the home screen.** A wide “What do you want to know?” field in
   the header. Typing a sentence works: filler words (“I want to know
   about…”) are ignored, plurals and one-letter slips still match
   (“napolean”, “dinosaur”).
2. **The room keeps the big subjects** a child recognises by picture: Space,
   Animals, Your Body, Earth, Plants, Science, Long Ago.
3. **Wings below the room** for subjects that have no exhibit yet. History is
   the first wing; each shows its icon, its name and how many books it holds.
   A wing appears only when it has books, so nothing on the menu is empty.
4. **The Time Machine** puts every article that happened at a time in order,
   with its date (“About 4,500 years ago”, “1815”) on the card.
5. **Today's question** stays one tap away.
6. **Big, readable targets.** Every target is at least 44 points; the home
   wings and the ask box are at least 56. Labels are words, not only icons.

## Wings still to open

A wing appears on the home screen as soon as its category has an article
and no exhibit; aim for three articles before opening one. In priority order from the stories above: **Famous People**,
**Machines & Inventions**, **Countries & Cultures**, **Art, Music & Sport**.
