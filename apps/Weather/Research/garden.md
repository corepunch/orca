# Gardeners' Weather — research

Gardeners already have plenty of planners and plant-care apps. They have no trusted, plain-English weather app that answers "frost tonight?", "do I need to water?" and "is the soil warm enough to sow?" for their own plot.
The niche is real but crowded with tiny, unproven frost apps; the winners combine early frost warnings, a rain-vs-evaporation water balance and soil temperature, with no data entry and no subscription trap.
The biggest gaps in the current edition are that it has no own location, no alerts, ground frost is under-warned, and nothing looks back at the rain that already fell.

Research date: November 2026. App Store prices and ratings are snapshots from listings and trackers. They vary by country and change often, so check them before quoting. Where a claim comes only from a developer's own listing, it is marked "(vendor claim)".

Notes:

- **The market splits in two.** Planners and journals (Seed to Spoon, GrowVeg, Seedtime, Gardenize, Planter) have big audiences but treat weather as a static frost date. Frost and weather apps (Frost Watch, My Cozy Plant, Frost Alert, NightFrost) read live weather but are new, with almost no ratings. Nobody clearly owns "the weather app for gardeners" yet.
- **Ground frost is the standout feature.** Frost Watch shows ground temperature beside air temperature. That matches the Met Office's advice: an app's overnight low is an air temperature, and on a clear, calm night 0–4 °C can still bring ground frost.
- **Pricing that people accept:** a one-off $2.99–4.99 (Gardenate, Frost Watch) or about $15–25 a year. Seed to Spoon's $46.99 a year and Seedtime's $84–168 a year draw complaints.
- **Rachio shows what goes wrong with automatic watering.** Its forum is full of "it watered after three days of rain" and "it skipped in a 100 °F week". Users trust it more when they can see why it skipped and can choose a nearby station.

## What people complain about

A caveat first. Reddit could not be fetched or searched directly (search engines returned no r/gardening, r/vegetablegardening or r/UKGardening threads, and reddit.com blocks automated fetches). The themes below come from App Store and Play reviews, the Rachio community forum, extension services and garden press. Before launch, someone should read the subreddits by hand to confirm them.

1. **Subscriptions and paywalls.** Users feel misled when a "free" app needs Premium for its core features. Free tiers are too small to plan a real garden (Seed to Spoon allows about 10 plants; GrowIt allows four in "planning to grow"). Trials turn into payments without clear notice. GrowVeg plans can no longer be opened once you stop paying. People ask for a one-time price. [Seed to Spoon reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/seed-to-spoon-garden-planner/id1312538762) · [GrowIt reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/6443580320/growit-vegetable-garden-care/reviews) · [Planta feedback summary](https://kimola.com/reports/comprehensive-planta-app-customer-feedback-report-app-store-us-155863) (AI-generated summary, treat as a weak signal) · [Gardenize Play reviews](https://grand-screen.com/apps/gardenize-garden-plant-care/reviews/) · [Gardenize, Gardenate and Planter in the Leaftide comparison](https://leaftide.com/learn/best-garden-planning-apps/)
2. **Advice that isn't local.** Calendars are fixed to one USDA zone, or tell you to sow cool-season crops in mid-summer even after you entered your frost dates. Static almanac dates are averages: the Almanac's own dates carry about a 30 % chance of frost after them. People outside the US have little that fits. [Veggie Garden Planner reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1329927332/veggie-garden-planner/reviews) · [Planter reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planter-garden-planner/id1542642210?see-all=reviews&platform=ipad) · [Almanac frost-date method](https://www.almanac.com/gardening/frostdates/ID/Twin%20Falls)
3. **The frost forecast said 3 °C and the plants froze anyway.** Forecast lows are air temperatures at about 2 m. Frost forms lower down, in low spots and frost pockets, on clear and still nights. Gardeners learn to fear any forecast near 35–42 °F. Generic apps don't explain this, and frost apps that do are unproven. [Met Office: forecasting frost](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/frost-and-ice/forecasting-frost) · [Patch, Redmond grower](https://patch.com/washington/redmond/quick-action-saves-root-connections-plants-from-frost-damage) · [Purdue frost report](https://www.purdue.edu/hla/sites/yardandgarden/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/1254) · [frost pockets](https://www.thegardenersalmanac.co.uk/Content/F/Frost%20%20pockets/Frost%20Pockets.htm)
4. **Frost warnings stop when frost doesn't.** NWS frost and freeze headlines run only in the "growing season", so early or late frosts go unflagged by apps that relay official warnings. [NWS frost/freeze program](https://www.weather.gov/media/bgm/frostfreeze.pdf)
5. **Watering advice that ignores the rain.** Planta's schedules don't move when you water early or late. Rachio users report watering after days of rain, skipping in heatwaves, and missing rain at their own house when the nearest station is wrong. They want to see why a skip happened. [Rachio: "rained for 3 days and still watered"](https://community.rachio.com/t/its-rained-for-3-days-and-rachio-still-watered/22121) · [Rachio: "don't trust weather intelligence"](https://community.rachio.com/t/i-dont-trust-weather-intelligence-and-climate-skip-at-all/25898) · [Rachio: "forecast lying means dead grass"](https://community.rachio.com/t/weather-forecast-lying-means-dead-grass/39639) · [Rachio: skips too random](https://community.rachio.com/t/rain-and-saturation-skip-too-random-to-be-of-any-use/41354)
6. **Too much setup and data entry.** People have to add every variety by hand, set zones and frost dates themselves, and jump between a planner app and a care app. Some have lost gardens after signing in. [Planter reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planter-garden-planner/id1542642210?see-all=reviews&platform=ipad) · [Veggie Garden Planner reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1329927332/veggie-garden-planner/reviews)
7. **Slow, buggy, or just a website.** Reviews mention freezes when logging several plants, "sooooo slow", and apps that only open a browser. [Garden Time Planner](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/garden-time-planner/id594225389) · [SmartGardener](https://mwm.ai/apps/smartgardener/1533099386)
8. **AI answers you can't trust.** Seed to Spoon's Growbot is called unreliable in reviews. [WhistleOut](https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/Apps/seed-to-spoon-app)
9. **Soil temperature is hard to find.** Lawn people time pre-emergent at about 55 °F (13 °C) at 4 in (10 cm) for several days, and use paid or niche tools (Syngenta GreenCast, SoilIQ) to get it. [UNL Extension](https://lancaster.unl.edu/soil-temperatures-and-spring-preemergence-herbicide-applications) · [GreenCast crabgrass](https://intercom.help/greencast-connect/en/articles/10296580-crab-grass-germination) · ["false spring" warning](https://yardmastery.com/blogs/lawn-care/false-spring-and-your-lawn-why-you-shouldnt-seed-or-apply-pre-emergent-yet-february-2026)

## Persona

### Primary: Margaret, the allotment vegetable grower

- **Context:** 61, semi-retired, in Leeds (UK). She has a 125 m² allotment 10 minutes from home and a small back garden with pots. She grows potatoes, beans, brassicas, courgettes, tomatoes in a cold frame, and dahlias. She has gardened for 20 years and knows what to do; she wants to know **when**.
- **Goals:**
  - not lose tender plants to a late frost in May or an early one in September;
  - not cart watering cans to the plot when rain is coming;
  - sow and plant out at the right soil temperature, not by the packet date;
  - catch potato blight weather early;
  - pick the right afternoon to spray, dig or put the fleece on.
- **Frustrations:**
  - the BBC app said 4 °C and her runner beans blackened;
  - garden apps want her to type in every plant and then ask for £40 a year;
  - US apps talk about "zone 7b" and inches;
  - she has to piece together rain from two apps and a gauge.
- **Devices:** an iPhone a few years old, with large text turned on. She uses the home-screen widget and notifications more than she opens apps. She has an iPad at home for planning on winter evenings.
- **Usage moments:**
  - 7 am at breakfast: should I go to the plot today, and what for?
  - 6–8 pm: frost tonight? Do I fleece or bring pots in?
  - Sunday evening: the week ahead for watering, sowing and the best dry day.
  - At the plot, outdoors, sometimes with gloves on: a quick glance at wind and rain for the next 3 hours.
  - In February and March: when can I start? Is the soil warming?

### Secondary: Dan, the suburban lawn and raised-bed homeowner

- **Context:** 38, outside Columbus, Ohio (USDA zone 6a). He has a lawn, two raised beds of tomatoes and peppers, and a sprinkler on a hose timer. He thinks in °F, inches and mph.
- **Goals:** time pre-emergent and overseeding by soil temperature; know whether to run the sprinkler this week; avoid mowing wet grass; know his frost dates.
- **Frustrations:** generic "1 inch a week" advice; smart controllers he doesn't trust; paywalled lawn tools.
- **Usage:** a weekend glance and alerts; summer evenings deciding whether to water.

### Tertiary (not designed for, noted)

Ornamental and container gardeners (heat on pots, wind on tall plants), and fruit growers (blossom frost, which needs earlier and harder frost thresholds).

## What we must avoid

- **Subscription traps:** a "free" download that is useless without paying, trials that convert silently, or features that lock when payment stops. Keep frost tonight and water this week free. Offer a one-off or a small yearly price, and say clearly what it buys.
- **Turning into a planner:** no plant databases, bed layouts or variety lists. Data entry is the second most common complaint, and planners are a crowded, different market. A few toggles, not a garden inventory.
- **Air-temperature-only frost:** this is the exact failure gardeners describe. Warn early on clear, still nights, and show the reasoning.
- **Alert fatigue:** no daily nagging, no alerts for things the user didn't choose, and an "all clear" instead of silence when a risk passes.
- **False precision:** reanalysis and model grids (about 2–11 km) can't see frost pockets. Say "possible" and "likely", round numbers, and say "about".
- **Unexplained automation:** Rachio's lesson is that a skip without a reason looks like a bug. Every verdict says why in one line, with the numbers behind it.
- **US-only framing:** "zones" and inches as the only language. The UK and Australia have strong gardening cultures. Use plain thresholds and local units.
- **Fear:** frost and storms are things to prepare for, framed calmly: what to do, and by when.
- **Folklore as science:** moon gardening, if added, is optional and labelled as tradition.
- **Accounts, tracking and selling location:** frost apps that win on trust say "no account, no tracking". Keep it that way.
- **The free Open-Meteo API in a commercial build:** see the licensing note above.
- **Slow and buggy:** answer in under a second from the cache. A glance at the plot must never wait on the network.
