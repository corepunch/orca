# Hikers' Weather — research

Hikers don't want another weather app. They want one answer: is it safe and worth going, and when must they be off the top?
The gaps competitors leave are summit-versus-trailhead forecasts, storm timing given as a time instead of "30 % PM", daylight left, plain words and a forecast that still works offline. Paywalls on safety data are a common source of anger.
Our scoring card and reasons already work. What is missing is the right height, a storm clock, place search, offline use, imperial units and a commercial Open-Meteo licence.

Researched November 2026. Prices and ratings come from App Store listings and third-party trackers. They vary by country and change often, so treat them as approximate. Reddit could not be fetched directly (the research tool is blocked there). Community evidence comes from 14ers.com, UKClimbing / UKHillwalking, Backpacking Light, the Windy community forum, Trustpilot and App Store listings.

Notes:

- **No one owns "plain-words verdict plus summit height plus storm clock".** Mountain-Forecast owns height bands but is a table. OpenSnow owns lightning timing but is ski-first, US-centred and $50–100/yr. PeakWeather and TrekDay are new and small, so the niche is open but being contested.
- **Hikers trust human interpretation.** They value the NWS forecast discussion, MWIS, SAIS and OpenSnow's written posts, and our plain-reason sentences are the closest automated equivalent.
- **Mapping apps (AllTrails, Gaia, CalTopo) put weather where planning happens.** We can't beat them on maps. We beat them by being the best second opinion, and by being fast.

## What people complain about

1. **The forecast is for the wrong height.** A point forecast's grid box can span 3,000–4,000 ft of relief. The 14ers.com guide says to check the elevation of the forecast point. Users report checking several nearby grid points. Summit wind is often described as 2–3× valley wind (a rule of thumb, not a measurement). The 14ers forum also says OpenSnow is right for summits but far off for the approach, which shows people need **both** heights.
   [14ers forecast guide](https://www.14ers.com/planning-weather-forecast), [14ers "accurate weather" thread](https://www.14ers.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=801201), [thenextsummit NWS guide](https://thenextsummit.org/how-to-read-a-mountain-weather-forecast-before-your-hike/), [simplyhike mountain weather](https://simplyhike.co.uk/blogs/blog/mountain-weather)
2. **Storm timing is vague.** "30 % chance of storms in the PM" is useless when lightning arrives at 8:30 am. Users want the hour, and the "be down by" time. Colorado has near-daily storms in July and August.
   [14ers forecast guide](https://www.14ers.com/planning-weather-forecast), [OpenSnow tips](https://opensnow.com/news/post/expert-tips-avoiding-thunderstorms-on-colorado-fourteeners), [NPS weather safety](https://home.nps.gov/subjects/healthandsafety/severe-weather-safety.htm)
3. **Mountain wind is unreliable.** UK users say yr.no's Cairngorm wind is "nowhere near" MWIS and Met Office figures. Even MWIS and Met Office diverge from observations, and 2-hourly mean wind hides the gusts that knock you over.
   [UKClimbing yr wind](https://www.ukclimbing.com/forums/winter_climbing/yrno_wind_speeds-697305), [UKHillwalking Met Office wind](https://www.ukhillwalking.com/forums/winter_climbing/met_office_wind_issues-789226), [UKHillwalking Windy and yr](https://www.ukhillwalking.com/forums/winter_climbing/windy_and_yrany_good_for_weather_predictions-755693)
4. **Apps disagree, so people check four.** Forum users run Mountain-Forecast, NOAA, OpenSnow, Windy and MWIS side by side. Some call Mountain-Forecast "useless" in Colorado, and others call MWIS pessimistic. Nobody tells them how confident the forecast is.
   [14ers thread](https://www.14ers.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=801201), [UKClimbing forecast variation](https://www.ukclimbing.com/forums/hill_talk/forecast_variation-761088), [UKHillwalking mountain forecasts](https://www.ukhillwalking.com/forums/winter_climbing/mountain_weather_forecasts-605193)
5. **Nothing works offline.** Backpackers save screenshots and screen recordings of meteograms before leaving. A Windy user found offline use worked in the browser but not the app. A satellite messenger is the fallback.
   [BPL new backcountry app thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/new-backcountry-weather-forecast-app-website/), [Windy offline](https://community.windy.com/topic/31212/off-line-use-of-the-app), [BPL satellite forecasts](https://backpackinglight.com/podcast-episode-132-satellite-messenger-weather-forcasts)
6. **Paywall creep on data people consider safety data.** Windy moved its 10-day forecast and radar history to Premium, and users called it bait-and-switch. AllTrails moved Trail Conditions to an $80 tier, and Trustpilot users describe removed features and upgrade nags. Mountain-Forecast's free tier has ads.
   [Windy "free items removed"](https://community.windy.com/topic/24068/free-items-removed?page=2), [Windy "10-day gone"](https://community.windy.com/topic/24089/10-day-forecast-gone/24), [AllTrails Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/alltrails.com?page=3), [AllTrails pop-ups and billing (aggregator)](https://mwm.ai/articles/app-updates/alltrails-v26-10-40-adds-intrusive-pop-ups-and-dark-billing-flows-october-2026)
7. **Clutter and dense meteograms.** meteoblue users asked to bring back a simpler all-in-one view. Windy is powerful but takes skill to read. People want to know what the cloud means (its height and type), not just a percentage.
   [meteoblue community](https://community.meteoblue.com/category/28/ios-app), [14ers thread](https://www.14ers.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=801201)
8. **Crashes and reliability in the app you rely on.** AllTrails' July 2026 offline-map crash is the example. A Mountain-Forecast review sample says the app "works for about 4 seconds" (aggregator, weak signal).
   [AllTrails crash report](https://mwm.ai/articles/alltrails-v26-7-40-crashes-on-offline-maps-sparking-user-backlash-in-july-2026), [JustUseApp Mountain-Forecast](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/6446204098/mountain-forecast-com/reviews)
9. **No daylight planning.** Several apps show first and last light, but none we found turns "start time plus hike length" into a turnaround time or warns "you'll finish after dark". (We found no thread that asks for this; it is an inferred gap.)
   [happiestoutdoors roundup](https://happiestoutdoors.ca/weather-apps-for-hiking/), [OpenRunner weather](https://openrunner.com/en/features/weather)

## Persona

**Primary: Maya, the weekend peak-bagger (day hiker).** She is 34 and a nurse who lives an hour from the mountains (Denver, Front Range; she could equally be a Manchester hillwalker heading to Snowdonia, or a Munich hiker in the Alps). She does 1–2 day hikes a week in season, 8–20 km, often above treeline.

- **Goals:** pick the best day this week; know whether to start at 5 am or 8 am; be off the ridge before storms; know whether she needs microspikes or a shell; have a view at the top.
- **Frustrations:** four apps that disagree; town forecasts; "30 % PM storms"; ads and paywalls; no signal at the trailhead; °C/m apps when she thinks in °F/ft (or the reverse in the UK).
- **Devices:** iPhone (cracked case, low-power mode on the trail), sometimes an Apple Watch, AllTrails or Gaia for the map.
- **Usage moments:** Tuesday night on the sofa ("which day?"); the evening before ("what time do I start?"); 5 am in the car at the trailhead, where signal fades ("still a go?"); on the summit at 11 am ("how long till it turns?").

**Secondary: Tom, the multi-day backpacker / hut trekker.** He is 52 and does 3–7 day trips (the John Muir Trail, Tour du Mont Blanc, Cairngorms) and plans weeks ahead.

- **Goals:** a trend for the whole trip; the cold overnight at camp height; a weather window for the high pass day.
- **Frustrations:** no offline forecast after day 1; 7-day horizons; one point per trip; data costly to get by satellite.
- **Devices:** iPhone plus a Garmin inReach.
- **Usage moments:** weeks before (when to go); the night before he goes off-grid (download everything); at a hut with Wi-Fi (refresh).

## Feature gaps

Ordered by priority. Sizes: **S** is days, **M** is about 1–2 weeks, **L** is several weeks. Open-Meteo facts come from [the forecast docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs), [the ensemble docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/ensemble-api), [the elevation API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/elevation-api) and [the elevation downscaling post](https://openmeteo.substack.com/p/improving-weather-forecasts-with).

### 0. Prerequisite: an Open-Meteo commercial licence (business, not code)

- **What:** The free API is **for non-commercial use only**. The terms list "websites or apps that have subscriptions or display advertisements" as commercial, and they cap free use at about 10,000 calls/day per IP and app.
- **Why:** A paid or subscription App Store app needs the paid API (`customer-` host plus `apikey`). It is unclear whether a free app with no ads or subscription counts as commercial. Ask Open-Meteo before launch.
- **Cost:** Prices are not listed on the terms page.
- **Files:** `forecast.lua` (base URL and key).

Source: [Open-Meteo terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms).

### 1. Summit and trailhead, two heights per place — P0, S–M

- **What:** Each hiking place carries a `summit` and a `trailhead` (lat, lon, elevation). Both come from one request. The page shows "top" and "start", and the verdict uses the worse of the two for the exposed hours.
- **Why:** This is complaint #1, and it fixes today's town and valley coordinates (Zermatt, Yosemite Valley, Lake Tahoe).
- **Data:** Open-Meteo takes comma-separated `latitude`, `longitude` and `elevation` and returns a list. `elevation=<m>` sets the downscaling height. Downscaling is documented for temperature; whether wind is downscaled is **uncertain**, which is why feature 2 exists. The Elevation API (90 m DEM) gives a default trailhead height.
- **Files:** `places.lua`, `forecast.lua` (`urls`, `parse` for a list response), `provider.lua`, `activities/hiking.lua`, `Pages/Activity.xml`, `Data/Activity.xml`.

### 2. Wind at summit height (free-air) — P0, M

- **What:** Interpolate wind speed and direction at the summit's height from pressure levels. Show "ridge wind" next to the 10 m gust, and use the stronger one for the ridge rules.
- **Why:** Mountain wind is the most criticised number. 10 m wind over a smoothed grid cell under-reads exposed ridges.
- **Data:** Fetch `wind_speed_850hPa`, `wind_speed_700hPa`, `wind_speed_600hPa` and `wind_speed_500hPa` with their `geopotential_height_*` and `temperature_*`, and interpolate by height. Note that free-air wind is an *upper bound* for a sheltered top. Say so in words.
- **Files:** `forecast.lua`, `activities/hiking.lua`.

### 3. Storm clock and "be down by" — P0, M

- **What:**
  - Today's first hour of meaningful thunder risk, as a clock time.
  - A recommended "off the summit by" time an hour or so before it.
  - A dedicated hourly storm row.
  - Early-start advice on storm days.
- **Why:** Vague "PM storms" is complaint #2, and OpenSnow charges $50+/yr for lightning bars.
- **Data:** Open-Meteo hourly `cape`, `lifted_index`, `convective_inhibition`, `showers`, `precipitation_probability` and WMO codes 95–99. `lightning_potential` exists, but only from ICON-D2 in Central Europe at 15-minute resolution. Optionally, the ensemble API gives the share of members with convective rain. The thresholds are heuristic and need tuning against observed storm days. Mark this **uncertain** until it is validated.
- **Files:** `activities/hiking.lua` (a new `storm(forecast)` helper), `score.lua` (a reusable "first hour where" helper), `report.lua`, `Pages/Activity.xml`.

### 4. Daylight budget and turnaround — P0, S–M

- **What:**
  - First and last light (civil twilight) and the daylight left from now.
  - Optional: you enter a start time and the hours to the summit. The app then gives the turnaround time as the earliest of (storm time, last light minus the descent time) and warns "after dark".
- **Why:** No competitor we found does the subtraction for you. It is cheap and memorable.
- **Data:** Daily `daylight_duration`, `sunrise` and `sunset`. Compute civil twilight locally (−6° solar altitude) in `sky.lua`, with no API needed.
- **Files:** `sky.lua`, `report.lua` (`Report.sun`), `activities/hiking.lua`, plus a small input sheet in `Pages/Activity.xml`.

### 5. Any peak: search, location, saved places — P0, L

- **What:** Search peaks by name, use device location, pin favourites, and add a place with a long-press on the map (later).
- **Why:** A fixed list of about 10 places can't be a real App Store app. Every competitor has search.
- **Data:**
  - The [Open-Meteo Geocoding API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/geocoding-api) (GeoNames-based, with feature codes). Whether it ranks peaks (`MT` / `PK`) well enough is **uncertain**.
  - An alternative is a bundled peak list from OpenStreetMap `natural=peak` (ODbL: needs attribution, and a share-alike derived database), or GeoNames (CC-BY).
  - Device location needs engine/iOS support. Check the ORCA UIKit plugin.
- **Files:** `places.lua` (data becomes user-editable), `provider.lua`, a new search page in `Pages/`, `App.lua`, persistence (the engine API is unknown).

### 6. Offline forecast with age stamp — P0, M

- **What:** Persist each place's last forecast to disk. Show it with "updated 3 h ago" when there is no network. Offer "download the trip" for planned days.
- **Why:** Hikers screenshot forecasts today, and offline use is a paid feature elsewhere.
- **Data:** None new.
- **Files:** `provider.lua` (cache to disk), `forecast.lua` (already pure parsing; store the raw JSON), `report.lua` (age line), `Pages/Hub.xml`.

### 7. Imperial units — P0, S–M

- **What:** °F, mph, ft, inches, following the region by default, with a toggle. The UK can mix mph with °C and metres.
- **Why:** The US is the largest market for this niche, and every 14er source is in feet.
- **Data:** Open-Meteo `temperature_unit`, `wind_speed_unit` and `precipitation_unit`, or convert locally. Converting locally is better because the rules work in metric.
- **Files:** `score.lua` (`fmt`), `report.lua`, `activities/hiking.lua` (metrics), settings.

### 8. Confidence: do the models agree? — P1, M

- **What:** A one-word confidence (sure / likely / uncertain) per day, from the spread across models or ensemble members, especially for storms and wind. Show 16 days with fading confidence.
- **Why:** People check four apps because they don't know how sure the forecast is.
- **Data:**
  - Option one is the `models=` parameter with several models (e.g. ECMWF IFS, GFS, ICON). The exact identifiers should be checked in the docs.
  - Option two is the ensemble API: ECMWF IFS 51 members to 15 days, GFS 31 members, and ICON-D2-EPS for 2 days in Europe.
  - Either option adds roughly one extra request per place.
- **Files:** `forecast.lua`, `score.lua`, `report.lua`.

### 9. Official warnings and avalanche links — P1, M

- **What:** Show active official warnings for the place, plus a link to the regional avalanche, MWIS or SAIS forecast in winter.
- **Why:** This is trusted, human-made data, and its absence looks negligent.
- **Data:**
  - US: [api.weather.gov alerts](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api) (free, public domain, with a required User-Agent; use a generic one).
  - Europe: MeteoAlarm feeds. Their licence terms are **uncertain** and need checking.
  - Avalanche: link out per region and don't scrape.
- **Files:** a new `alerts.lua`, `provider.lua`, `report.lua`.

### 10. Snow, ice and recent rain — P1, S

- **What:**
  - Snow depth at the summit.
  - Snowfall over the last 72 h.
  - A morning freeze and afternoon thaw note.
  - Heavy rain in the last 24 h ("streams high").
- **Why:** It answers "microspikes or not?". AllTrails sells this as Trail Conditions.
- **Data:** Hourly `snow_depth` and `snowfall` with `past_days=3`, plus `precipitation` history.
- **Files:** `forecast.lua` (past days and new fields), `activities/hiking.lua`.

### 11. Smoke, UV and heat — P1, S

- **What:**
  - An AQI and PM2.5 smoke warning.
  - UV at altitude and over snow.
  - Heat stress from wet-bulb temperature.
- **Why:** Western US summers bring smoke, and desert or canyon hikes bring heat.
- **Data:**
  - The [Open-Meteo Air Quality API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/air-quality-api): `us_aqi`, `european_aqi`, `pm2_5`, and pollen in Europe.
  - Forecast API `wet_bulb_temperature_2m` and the existing `uv_index`.
- **Files:** `forecast.lua` (a third optional request like marine), `activities/hiking.lua`.

### 12. Trips and plan reminders — P1, L

- **What:**
  - A saved plan (peak, date, start time) or a trip (dates and stops).
  - A notification the evening before and on the morning of: go, start earlier, or postpone.
  - A "next good day" suggestion.
- **Why:** Turns a lookup app into a habit, and it is the best reason to subscribe (see "must avoid").
- **Data:** Existing data. Local notifications and background refresh need engine/iOS support (**unknown**).
- **Files:** a new `plans.lua`, `App.lua`, `provider.lua`, new pages.

### 13. Cloud base and "in the cloud at the top" — P2, S

- **What:** Estimate the cloud base from temperature and dew point, then say whether the summit will be in cloud.
- **Why:** It is the view question, plus navigation in fog.
- **Data:** `temperature_2m` and `dew_point_2m` at the trailhead (base ≈ 125 m × spread °C, a rough rule), plus `cloud_cover_low`.
- **Files:** `activities/hiking.lua`.

### 14. Compare peaks for a day — P2, M

- **What:** A row per pinned peak for a chosen day, with a verdict and window.
- **Why:** "Where should I go Saturday?"
- **Data:** A multi-location request.
- **Files:** a new hub section or page, `report.lua`.

### 15. Widget and Watch glance — P2, L

- **What:** The next plan's verdict, storm time and turnaround.
- **Data:** Existing data.
- **Files:** Engine-dependent (WidgetKit or watchOS are probably outside ORCA today).

## What we must avoid

- **Claiming the summit when it's the town.** Never label a valley forecast as a peak (today Zermatt, Yosemite Valley and Lake Tahoe are low points). Always print the height.
- **False safety.** A "good" verdict is not permission. Never imply that the app guarantees it is safe. One calm line ("weather changes fast in the mountains; turn back early") beats a nag screen. Keep the "wonder, not fear" tone, but never hide a cap such as storms, the dark or gusts.
- **False precision.** No "0 % storm risk", no minute-exact storm times, and heuristics labelled as estimates. Say "about".
- **Paywalling safety.** Storms, wind, cold, dark and warnings stay free. Paid features can be convenience: trips, many saved places, widgets, 16-day, offline trip download. Windy and AllTrails show the backlash when free features are taken away later, so decide the free line at launch and keep it.
- **Ads and dark patterns.** No ads, no upgrade pop-ups on launch, easy cancellation (the AllTrails October 2026 backlash).
- **Breaking Open-Meteo's terms.** Using the free API in a commercial app breaks them. Budget the paid API, and keep request counts low (one combined request per place, then cache).
- **Meteogram clutter.** Don't become Windy or meteoblue. Keep the verdict and reasons first, with charts second and at most two per view.
- **Silent staleness.** An offline forecast must always show its age. After about 12–24 h, it should say it is old.
- **A summit-only view.** Lower elevations matter for the approach (OpenSnow's weakness). Show both heights.
- **Competing on maps.** Don't build a trail map to rival AllTrails or Gaia. Link or import instead.
- **Scraping or bundling unlicensed data** (MWIS text, avalanche bulletins, lightning networks such as Blitzortung, which is non-commercial). Link to them instead.
- **Unverified "astonishing" claims in copy.** Rules of thumb (summit wind 2–3× the valley, cloud base 125 m/°C) must be phrased as rough guides.
