# Wondertown Character Design Bible

## Locked visual direction

Wondertown uses polished 2D animated-feature character art inside a warm,
slightly theatrical children’s storybook world. Characters have crisp dark
outlines, readable silhouettes, flat local colour, and graphic cel-shaded
shadow shapes. Use one or two controlled shadow planes per form rather than
soft gradients or painterly modelling. Highlights are warm; shadows lean
blue-green. Materials should remain legible through shape and colour, not
realistic surface texture.

These shading rules govern characters and model sheets, not the amount of
design or painted detail in the environment. Scene backgrounds may use rich
timber grain, aged metal, atmospheric depth and selective painterly edges;
keep the cast readable within them. For 3D-blockout overpaints, follow the Book
process document's drawing instructions: the blockout locks geography, not
the final prop designs or material finish.

The cast should feel like a collection of different toy traditions. Do not
make every character a small human in a different costume.

## Pip — final model

Pip is a small, friendly **workshop apprentice boy**. He has a soft compact
storybook silhouette, a brown padded newsboy/work cap, tousled brown hair, a
warm childlike face, a cream rolled-sleeve shirt, a brown leather workshop
apron with pockets, dark green cropped trousers, striped socks, and sturdy
brown lace-up boots. His apron carries ordinary hand tools.

Important change: Pip has **no winding mechanism**. Do not draw a key, gears,
springs, exposed clockwork, a circular gear emblem, or mechanical parts on his
body or clothing. The workshop’s magic exists in the world and its toys; Pip
does not need a mechanism on his body.

## Cel-shading rules

- Use clean 2D linework with a consistent medium-weight contour.
- Keep local colours mostly flat and separate materials with clear colour
  boundaries.
- Add hard-edged cel shadows, generally one main shadow family plus a small
  accent shadow; avoid airbrushed transitions.
- Use warm cream light and cool muted blue-green shadow shapes.
- Keep eyes, facial expressions, and silhouettes highly readable at thumbnail
  size.
- Avoid painterly brush texture, glossy 3D rendering, photorealistic material
  detail, and excessive surface noise.
- Apply this same rendering treatment to Bertrand, Old Tick, Marzipan, Nutmeg,
  and every future expression or pose sheet.

### Shared style prompt

> Polished 2D animated-feature character art for a premium children’s
> storybook, crisp clean dark outlines, flat local colours, graphic restrained
> cel shading with one or two hard-edged shadow planes, warm cream key light,
> cool blue-green shadows, readable expressive silhouettes, simple controlled
> shapes, no painterly gradients, no visible brush texture, no glossy CGI, no
> photorealism, no anime styling, no crosshatching.

## Bertrand — selected model

Bertrand is a **tall classic wooden nutcracker guard**. His body is carved
walnut with a slightly exaggerated square jaw, painted cherry-red uniform,
dark green details, brass trim, cream gloves, and a tall military hat. His legs
are long and rigid, his chest is broad, and his jaw makes the strongest
silhouette cue. His facial expression is permanently self-important rather
than threatening.

## Old Tick — selected model

Old Tick is a **living wall cuckoo clock with little wooden legs**. His warm
walnut case has a peaked top, an ivory clock face, expressive hands, a small
cuckoo door that works like a mouth, a visible pendulum, and two hanging
weights. His legs are short and slightly crooked. The clock face carries the
character’s eyes without becoming a human face pasted onto a clock.

## Marzipan — selected model

Marzipan is a **lopsided quilt-patchwork rag doll**. Her body is soft and
slightly asymmetrical, assembled from faded quilt squares in dusty rose,
mustard, aqua, cream, and plum. She has yarn hair, one button eye, a stitched
mouth, visible seams, and a simple pinafore shape. The asymmetry is gentle and
endearing, never horror-coded.

## Nutmeg — selected model

Nutmeg is a **small worn plush fox with floppy ears**. Her russet-orange plush
fur is faded at the edges, with cream muzzle and belly patches, a large soft
tail, expressive triangular ears, and a weathered dusty-teal scarf. A simple

## Consistency rules for the future artbook

- Keep each character’s construction fixed across expressions and poses.
- Pip remains the apprentice boy specified above, Bertrand remains carved
  wood, Old Tick remains a clock, Marzipan remains quilt cloth, and Nutmeg
  remains plush.
- Keep Pip’s selected brown cap, cream shirt, leather apron, green trousers,
  striped socks, boots and muted teal neckerchief stable; vary expression and
  pose, not costume identity. Do not restore the superseded tin body or uniform.
- Use a shared eye language: large readable eyes, simple highlights, no anime
  sparkle overload.
- Use restrained cel shading and warm highlights against cool shadows.
- For expression sheets, show neutral, curious, pleased, worried, angry,
  embarrassed, sad, and determined.
- For turnaround sheets, use front, three-quarter, side, and back views with
  the same proportions and material cues.
- Keep the background quiet and warm cream so silhouettes can be compared.
